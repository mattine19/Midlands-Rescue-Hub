03 / Request lifecycle

From need to placement

A high-level reading of the operations represented by the facade, matching engine, and dispatch service.

STEP 01

Register request

An organization submits an animal and urgency level, creating a PlacementRequest.

STEP 02

Check suitability

Volunteer attributes such as capacity, animal compatibility, availability, and medical-case eligibility inform fit.

STEP 03

Rank candidates

MatchingEngine returns ranked VolunteerScore entries with a score and match reason.

STEP 04

Dispatch a wave

DispatchService coordinates outreach to a selected group and can send SMS notifications.

STEP 05

Record response

Acceptance updates the request flow so the placement can progress toward fulfillment.

04 / Component notes

Design decisions worth calling out

These expandable notes work well for someone reviewing the project quickly from a portfolio or LinkedIn link.

Why use a facade?

RescueHubFacade provides a single coordination point for higher-level actions, rather than requiring a caller to manage every list and service directly.

Why rank volunteers?

MatchingEngine separates candidate ranking from dispatch. A score plus a human-readable match reason can make the shortlist easier to review.

Why dispatch in waves?

DispatchService exposes a wave-based operation, supporting controlled outreach rather than contacting every potential foster at once.

Why use enums?

AnimalType, RequestUrgency, RequestStatus, and OrganizationTier make key categories explicit and consistent in the model.

Why singleton lists?

RequestList, VolunteerList, and OrganizationList are marked as Singleton in the UML, indicating a shared access point for each collection in this design.

What is the scope of this page?

This is a static architecture showcase of the UML design. It does not implement volunteer accounts, live matching, SMS delivery, or a production database.
